# What "on track" means

From James's interview on 2026-11-11. On track is a ladder: meet one level, then move up to the next. The [monthly review](../playbooks/monthly-review.md) checks the level with a query.

## The levels

1. **Stable.** No bill is a month behind, and no bank account goes negative. Goal: 3 months in a row.
2. **Growing.** Bill Pay and the Emergency Fund both end the month higher than they started, instead of shrinking or going negative. Goal: 3 months in a row.
3. **Cushion.** The Emergency Fund holds a set amount, and Bill Pay never dips below a set amount. Amounts not chosen yet. Ask James when Level 2 is close.

## A missed month (any level)

- The streak restarts at zero. No verdict, just the count ([rule 9](../constitution.md)).
- At the next payday, name the cause in one line: a one-off (a life event, logged with `log_life_event`) or a pattern (the same bill or category twice).
- One-off: no change, keep going.
- Pattern: one fix, the smallest that works ([rule 15](../constitution.md)). Move a due date, cut one subscription, or shift where a payment comes from.
- Extra debt payments pause until the accounts are back above zero. Minimums never pause.

## Something big hits at Level 2 or 3

- Log it as a life event, and respond to the person first ([rule 7](../constitution.md)).
- Using the Emergency Fund for a real emergency is what it's for, not a failure.
- Drop back one level, not to the start.
- After one clean month at the lower level, pick back up where they were.

A bad month costs a month, not the whole plan.

## Not part of on track

- The 401k ([goals.md](goals.md)).
- How fast debt is shrinking. That's measured against the two-year goal ([debt-plan.md](debt-plan.md)), not this monthly test.
